What we checked, and what we didn't

Every cell in the table below was read by hand from Informative Annex C, Table C.1, printed page 70-778 of the 2023 NEC, and matched against what our engine computes. The conduit dimension tables in Chapter 9 have not been through that process yet — so if you work a fill percentage by hand from areas rather than taking the count from Annex C, you are resting on something we haven't verified. The fill limits, and why the count already includes them

Chapter 9, Table 1 sets the maximum percentage of a raceway's interior that conductors may occupy: 53% for a single conductor, 31% for exactly two, and 40% for three or more. The two-conductor figure is lower than the three-conductor figure, which surprises people — it's about jamming, not about space.

Annex C tables are computed from those limits for the case where every conductor is the same size. So a count read out of Annex C is already compliant with Table 1, and working the percentage separately is a cross-check rather than a second requirement.

When Annex C does not apply

  • Mixed conductor sizes. Annex C assumes they're all the same. Mix them and you work it by area from Chapter 9, Table 5 and Table 4.
  • A nipple 24 inches or shorter. Chapter 9, Note 4 permits 60% fill in that case.
  • Different insulation. XHHW and THHN have different areas. This table is THHN/THWN/THWN-2 only.
  • Derating. Fill is a physical limit. More than three current-carrying conductors in a raceway is an ampacity question and it is a separate calculation.

The mistake worth knowing

A common one: reading the 40% column of Chapter 9, Table 4 as though it were the conduit's total area. For 1/2" EMT the total interior is 0.304 in² and the 40% figure is 0.122 in². Divide your conductor area by the 40% figure instead of the total and every answer comes out roughly two and a half times too high — which reads as a failure on a run that's comfortably fine.
